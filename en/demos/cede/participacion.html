Public participation

We consult before deciding. Then we answer.

What makes a consultation credible is not opening it: it is closing the loop. When the period ends, a reasoned response to every submission received is published.

01How to take part

Four steps, each one on the record.

01

Read the file

The full draft, its technical note and the timetable are published when the period opens.

02

Make your submission

Through the online form, at a local round table, or in writing to the Technical Secretariat.

03

Get a receipt

Every submission is logged with a file number you can use to follow it.

04

Read the reasoned response

When it closes, what went into the final text, what did not, and why, is published submission by submission.

03Mechanisms

Four ways to have a say.

01

Public consultations

Every education policy of national scope goes out to consultation before it is approved. The full file is published when the period opens, and when it closes a reasoned response to every submission is published.

02

Forums and round tables

Working forums with a fixed calendar and public minutes, where the system's institutions and local organisations prepare the technical files.

03

Surveys

Short surveys of school communities on specific questions, with the results published in the open data catalogue.

04

Public hearings

Open sessions at which an organisation or an individual can address the Council directly, with advance registration and a public record.
